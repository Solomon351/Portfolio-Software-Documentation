Mega Chicken — Voice of the Customer

A sentiment, emotion and brand-perception audit of 108 public customer comments collected across TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, X and YouTube, spanning 2016–2026.

Executive Summary

01

Mega Chicken carries a fiercely loyal, taste-driven customer base — its Yamarita is close to a cult product — but the brand is quietly exposed on food safety and consistency, concentrated at specific branches, most visibly Lekki.

Are customers happy?

Broadly, yes — 56.5% of comments are positive vs. 25% negative, and loyalty language ("no eatery beats," "my number one") recurs across six years.

Overall sentiment

Net-positive but polarized. Praise clusters around specific dishes; complaints cluster around specific branches and food-safety lapses.

Defining emotion

Joy / craving. The dominant emotional register is delight in specific menu items (Yamarita, meat pie, jollof) rather than general brand affinity.

Branch Performance

04

Branch-level quality is inconsistent — the single biggest theme distinguishing happy customers from unhappy ones is which outlet they visited, not whether they like the brand.

Mentions by Branch (sentiment split)

Best-performing

Ikota Villa Estate ("the best megachicken"), Ajah and Ikeja draw consistent, unprompted praise.

Weakest

Lekki is the most-flagged branch: spoiled food, taste inconsistency, and a direct call to shut it down. One commenter separately rated Elf/Lekki highly for quality while other Lekki mentions are strongly negative — pointing to inconsistency between sub-locations or over time, not a uniformly bad branch.

Expansion demand

Unprompted requests for outlets in Victoria Island, Surulere, Abuja, Port Harcourt and Bayelsa — demand is outrunning footprint.

Strengths, Weaknesses, Risk & Opportunity

07

Biggest strength

An extremely strong, emotionally-loyal core menu — Yamarita above all — that customers crave, miss, and actively evangelize for, sustained across a full decade of comments (2016–2026).

Biggest weakness

Inconsistent execution of food safety and seasoning basics (oil, sugar/Maggi levels, spoiled or reused-looking product), concentrated enough at specific branches (notably Lekki) to generate repeat, specific complaints.

Greatest risk

Quiet customer defection to Item 7 and Chicken Republic — not because rivals taste better, but because Mega Chicken's own inconsistency (queues, reheated food, quality drops) opens the door.

Greatest opportunity

Codify and market the signature items (Yamarita, meat pie, jollof) as a formal "classics" line, while using unmet demand (VI, Surulere, out-of-Lagos requests) to guide expansion.

Recommendations

08
0–30 days

Branch-level food-safety spot checks, starting with Lekki

Address the recurring spoiled/rotten-food and reused-oil complaints directly — these are reputational-risk comments, not taste preferences, and they repeat across multiple years at the same location.

3–12 months

Standardize seasoning and queue management

Cap and standardize sugar/Maggi levels across branches to resolve the most common taste complaint, and introduce a queue/order-ahead system — customers are explicitly naming Chowdeck as the beneficiary of Mega Chicken's queue friction.

1–5 years

Expand footprint around proven demand

Prioritize new outlets in Victoria Island and Surulere, and evaluate presence outside Lagos (Abuja, Port Harcourt) — all directly requested by customers — anchored by the signature-items marketing platform.
